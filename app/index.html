Australia, by the numbers

One page of the statistics that describe the country: how many people live here, how the economy is travelling, how long we live, how our children learn, and how we power our homes. Every figure comes from an official release, and every figure carries its date.

At a glance

Australia's population passed 27.6 million in mid-2025, growing 1.5% in a year — mostly from migration. The economy grew just 1.4% in 2024–25, its weakest year since the early 1990s outside COVID, while unemployment stayed low at 4.3%. Australians live among the longest in the world, and renewables now supply more than a third of our electricity.

What changed most recently

Western Australia was the fastest-growing state (+2.2% in the year to June 2025); Tasmania the slowest (+0.2%). Inflation eased to 3.2% in the year to the September quarter 2025. And in 2024, dementia became the leading cause of death for the first time, overtaking heart disease.

Sources: ABS National, state and territory population June 2025; ABS CPI September quarter 2025; ABS Causes of Death 2024.

Population

Australia's estimated resident population was 27,614,400 at 30 June 2025 — up 420,100 people (1.5%) on the year before. Growth came mostly from net overseas migration (305,569 people), with natural increase (births minus deaths) adding 114,556.

What drove the growth

Net overseas migration supplied about 73% of the year's growth. Every state and territory grew, but Western Australia grew fastest (+2.2%) and Tasmania slowest (+0.2%).

ABS, National, state and territory population, June 2025.

Age and families

The median age is 38.5 years. The total fertility rate was 1.48 babies per woman in 2024 — the lowest since the ABS series began in 1924.

ABS, Population projections; ABS, Births, Australia, 2024.

Economy

The economy grew 1.4% in real terms in 2024–25. In the June quarter 2025 it rose 0.6%, and 1.8% through the year. GDP was about US$1,840 billion in 2025 (IMF), or US$71,934 per person on a purchasing-power basis (World Bank).

Jobs and prices

Unemployment 4.3% (October 2025), with 67.0% of working-age people in the labour force. Inflation 3.2% in the year to the September quarter 2025; the RBA's preferred underlying measure, the trimmed mean, was 3.3% in October 2025.

Households saved 4.2% of their income in the June quarter 2025, down from 5.2% the quarter before.

ABS, Labour Force October 2025; ABS, CPI September quarter 2025; ABS, National Accounts June quarter 2025.

What Australia sells

Total goods and services exports were A$645.9 billion in 2024. China bought A$196.3 billion of them — about 30% — followed by the United States (A$92.8bn) and Japan (A$75.7bn).

DFAT, Australia's trade factsheet — goods & services, calendar 2024.

What the economy is made of

Services dominate — about two-thirds of value added. Australia was the world's largest iron ore producer in 2024, its second-largest gold producer, and the world's third-largest LNG exporter.

World Bank, 2025; DFAT / DISR, 2024. "Other" (6.3%) is the residual of the published sector shares.

One customer, three in ten dollars

China bought A$196.3 billion of Australian goods and services in 2024 — about 30% of all exports, more than the United States and Japan combined. That concentration is why trade with China is the first number in every Australian economic debate.

Health

Australians live long lives — but not equally long. Life expectancy at birth in 2022–2024 was 81.1 years for males and 85.1 years for females, among the highest in the world.

Life expectancy at birth (years)

ABS, Life expectancy 2022–2024 (all Australians and non-Indigenous); First Nations figures 2020–2022.

The gap that remains

First Nations males born in 2020–2022 could expect to live to 71.9 years and First Nations females to 75.6 years — roughly 9 years less than non-Indigenous Australians. Closing that gap is the largest unfinished task in Australian health statistics.

There were 187,268 deaths registered in 2024, a crude death rate of 6.9 per 1,000 people. The median age at death was 82.

ABS, Life expectancy 2022–2024; ABS, Deaths, Australia, 2024.

A first in 2024. Dementia — including Alzheimer's disease — became Australia's leading cause of death, overtaking ischaemic heart disease for the first time since records began.

Source: ABS, Causes of Death, Australia, 2024.

Education

In 2024, 4,132,006 students were enrolled in 9,653 schools across Australia — up 1.1% on 2023. They were taught by 320,377 full-time equivalent teachers, a student-to-teacher ratio of 12.9 to one, the lowest since 2006.

Who runs the schools

Government schools teach nearly two-thirds of students. Independent-school enrolments have grown 30.7% since 2014, far faster than government schools.

ABS, Schools, 2024; ACARA, National Report on Schooling in Australia 2024.

Staying on at school

79.9% of students stayed on to Year 12 in 2024, up from 79.1% in 2023. Of the 4.13 million students, 2.27 million (54.9%) were in primary school and 1.86 million (45.1%) in secondary school.

98.6% of 6- to 15-year-olds were enrolled in school in 2024.

ABS, Schools, 2024; ACARA, National Report on Schooling in Australia 2024.

Energy

Renewable sources supplied 37% of Australia's domestic electricity use in 2024–25, up from 34% the year before — the highest share on record. Renewable electricity production rose 9.7% to 372 petajoules.

Renewable electricity, 2024–25

Solar produced 191 PJ (+11% on the year) and wind 130 PJ (+15%). Hydro and other renewables supplied the remaining 51 PJ.

ABS, Energy Account, Australia, 2024–25. Hydro & other is the residual of the ABS total.

The bigger picture

Renewables were 10.1% of all energy consumed in 2024–25 — electricity is greening far faster than transport and industry. The Australian Government's target is for renewables to supply 82% of electricity by 2030.

Coal still generated about half of Australia's electricity in 2024, down from 61% a decade ago.

DCCEEW, Australian Energy Statistics 2024–25; Australian Government, Powering Australia plan.

    Methods & notes

    Population figures are the ABS estimated resident population. Economic growth is in chain volume (real) terms. Life expectancy uses three years of deaths data. School figures count full-time plus part-time students; teachers are full-time equivalent. Energy figures are for the financial year 2024–25 unless stated.
